Start here · plain English

Four words you will use all the time.

Metric

A number you track

Example: post reach, video views, comments, or clicks.

KPI

The number tied to success

The metric that best tells you whether a business goal is being achieved.

Benchmark

A point of comparison

Example: last month, the account average, a target, or industry norm.

Insight

An explanation that leads to action

It connects what happened, why it matters, and what to do next.

You do not need to memorize every metric. Start by understanding what the number means and what decision it can support.

Start here · reading a report

How to read four basic
social-media metrics.

👁️
Reach

How many unique people saw it

One person counts once, even if they saw the post many times.

🔁
Impressions

How many times it appeared

The same person can create multiple impressions.

💬
Engagements

How people interacted

Likes, comments, shares, saves, clicks, replies, and taps.

⚖️
Engagement rate

Interaction relative to delivery

Engagements compared with reach or impressions.

Simple rule: Reach tells you who saw it. Impressions tell you how often. Engagement tells you what people did.

Types of data

Social data has
two languages.

Structured

📋 Quantitative data

  • Typically tabular: CSV, Excel, XML
  • Stored in spreadsheets, relational databases, or data warehouses
  • Searchable and analyzable using Excel, SQL, and BI platforms
  • Examples: likes, shares, views, impressions, reach
VS
Unstructured

💬 Qualitative data

  • Formats include PDFs, images, audio, video, and emails
  • Typically stored as native files or documents in data lakes
  • Valuable information that is harder to analyze with common tools
  • Examples: conversations, mentions, reviews, posts, sentiment
Structured data tells you how much happened. Unstructured data adds the context around why.

Four techniques

Four questions turn data into decisions.

Descriptive

What happened?

KPI reporting, visualization, exploratory analysis, profiling.

Social: likes or shares a post received.

Diagnostic

Why happened?

Explanatory analysis, regression, root-cause analysis, hypothesis testing.

Social: why one post outperformed.

Predictive

What next?

Forecasting, machine learning, regression, classification.

Social: potential engagement from trends.

Prescriptive

So what?

Scenario testing, generative AI, reinforcement learning, recommendations.

Social: best time to post for engagement.

Data-driven insights · organic example

From performance update
to organic insight.

What happened

Impressions increased 30%

Organic content visibility rose week over week.

+80K incremental impressions
Why it may have happened

The content mix changed.

The pattern suggests that diversified formats and more video with human elements — faces, voices, and real experiences — resonated more strongly with users.

More videoHuman presenceFormat diversityHigher resonance
So what

Build on the winning pattern.

Prioritize human-led video in the content mix, keep testing hooks and formats, and track reach, saves, shares, and completion rate.

Insight: Organic impressions rose 30% after content diversified toward video with human elements. The pattern suggests audiences responded more strongly to relatable, people-centered storytelling. Next: validate this through repeated tests across topics and formats.

Analytical discipline Next-level

A pattern is not automatically proof.

Correlation

“Thursday posts had higher reach.”

This is a useful pattern. It may still reflect creative, audience, paid support, timing, or outside events.

Causation

“Thursday posting caused higher reach.”

This needs stronger evidence: comparable samples, controlled experiments, or properly designed testing.

Descriptive statistics

Descriptive statistics help
profile the data. Next-level

They help you explore and understand the key characteristics of a dataset before assuming a performance change is meaningful.

Central tendency

Mean and median describe typical performance.

Spread

Minimum, maximum, range, and standard deviation show variability.

Distribution

Percentiles, outliers, missing values, and skew reveal what averages hide.

Before saying “this content performed well,” ask: Compared with what is typical?
Before analysis, check: completeness · consistent metric definitions · duplicates · correct date range · comparable segments and channels.
