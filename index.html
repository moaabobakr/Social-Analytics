Why it matters

What happens without social media analytics?

You cannot track performance clearly
You do not know what content worked, what failed, what drove reach, or what improved engagement over time.
You do not know what people are saying
Without listening, you miss how audiences perceive your brand, products, service quality, and campaign messaging.
You cannot explain problems early
If a product or service goes wrong, you cannot easily identify where the drop happened or what sentiment changed.
You lose decision confidence
Teams start guessing instead of using evidence to improve strategy, content, and community decisions.

Framework Core

The Three Pillars

📈
Performance
Measures how the brand’s owned channels and content perform over time.
🎧
Listening
Analyzes what audiences say about the brand, products, and category online.
🧭
Reporting
Turns data into a clear story with context, conclusions, and next actions.

Pillar 1: Performance

Performance shows how social accounts behave during a reporting period and whether content delivered against its intended objective.

  • • Measures traction across reach, engagement, growth, and content efficiency.
  • • Tests whether audience interaction reflected campaign or content goals.
  • • Reveals how the brand is presenting itself through platform behavior and content choices.
  • • Creates the foundation for benchmarks and future recommendations.
Core Questions
Did the content achieve its intended objective?
Which formats and pillars generated the strongest response?
How did results shift versus prior periods or benchmarks?

Pillar 2: Listening

What it does

Listening captures online conversations, keywords, and mentions to understand perception, recurring themes, and sentiment momentum around the brand.

Sentiment model
😊
Positive
😐
Neutral
☹️
Negative
Themes: product, service, pricing, campaigns
Sources: X, comments, forums, news
Method: automated NLP + manual review
Output: perception insights and risks

Pillar 3: Reporting

Reporting combines performance and listening into a full story: what changed, why it changed, and what the brand should do next.

What changed: key shifts in metrics, volume, sentiment, and audience behavior.
Why it changed: content mix, media support, timing, seasonality, competitors, or platform changes.
What to do next: practical recommendations for content, targeting, cadence, and messaging.
Story formula
Data → Insight → Action

A useful report does not stop at numbers. It explains what they mean and what should happen next.

Unique audience reached and total views, including repeat exposure.
Interaction
Engagements
Likes, comments, shares, saves, clicks, replies, taps.

Engagement relative to reach or impressions.
Video
Views & Completion
Video views, watch depth, story completion and exits.
Activity
Posting Cadence
Number of posts, format mix, and frequency by platform.

How Metrics Are Collected

Manual collection
  • • Native platform insights for accuracy checks and spot analysis.
  • • Manual coding for sentiment quality control and nuanced categories.
  • • Useful for smaller scopes, audits, and verification.
Tool-based collection
  • • Emplifi for performance metrics.
  • • Talkwalker for monitoring, listening, and sentiment.
  • • Best for recurring reports, cross-platform dashboards, and trend tracking.
Best practice: automate collection where possible, then review outputs manually before reporting them to clients or leadership.

Monthly Deck Skeleton

1. Methodology and tools used
2. Executive summary: performance + sentiment highlights
3. Performance scorecard
4. Performance per each platform
5. Followers, posting cadence, top posts, content pillars
6. Media views and trend analysis
7. Listening scorecard and sentiment sampling
8. Community overview and recommendations

What a Social Media Analyst Does

🧮
Collects data
Pulls account, content, and audience data from tools and native insights.
🔎
Interprets patterns
Finds what is changing, what is working, and what it means for the brand.
💡
Recommends action
Turns evidence into next-step guidance for content, community, and strategy.
A strong analyst does more than count likes and shares. They connect social data to business questions, content quality, audience understanding, and future planning.

Skills for Junior Analysts

  • • Curiosity and analytical thinking
  • • Attention to detail
  • • Clear communication and storytelling
Technical skills
  • • Clean and manipulate data
  • • Build charts and dashboards
  • • Structure clear reporting decks
  • • Work with analytics and listening tools
